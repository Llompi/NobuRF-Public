NobuRF and openEMS compared

Both are free, open-source FDTD solvers. openEMS is installed on your computer, scripted from Python, MATLAB or Octave, and runs on the CPU. NobuRF runs in a browser tab on your GPU, and I check it against openEMS. On the same patch antenna, meshed the same way, the two land within 0.7 % of each other on resonance.

If your design is copper on a board, NobuRF gets you from an idea to an S11 curve without installing anything. If it has 3D bodies, such as a horn, a cavity filter or a connector you want to model in full, use openEMS. NobuRF cannot draw 3D bodies yet.

How close the results are

I ran one patch antenna in both codes: a 16 × 12.4 mm patch on 1.5 mm FR-4 (εr 4.4, no loss), fed by a microstrip line through a 50 Ω lumped port, with zero-thickness copper. Both used a graded mesh with 0.25 mm cells at the copper. openEMS v0.37.0 placed its mesh lines a third of a cell inside each copper edge, as its authors advise for thin metal.

Both are FDTD codes, so this checks that NobuRF implements the method correctly. It does not check the method against a real board. For that, the validation report compares NobuRF with published measurements: the patches from Chang et al. (1986) land within 4 % of their measured resonance on substrates up to 0.11 wavelengths thick, and further off on thicker ones. A line-fed patch from Sheen et al. (1990) lands 0.3 % low. I haven't measured a board of my own on a VNA yet.

Where they disagree

The coarse grid in the live demo. The demo in the app runs on a uniform 0.5 mm grid to keep it fast. On that grid NobuRF reads 0.4 to 1.3 % high against openEMS on its graded mesh. The main cause is the substrate: 0.5 mm cells put only three cells across 1.5 mm, where the graded runs use six.

The inset notch on a uniform grid. On a uniform 0.25 mm grid the inset patch's dip read −36.8 dB in NobuRF against −15.6 dB in openEMS. The notch edges were snapping to the grid. With mesh lines on the notch edges, NobuRF reads −16.3 dB and the impedance curves agree to 11 Ω on average.

The port model. This matters more than the mesh. In openEMS alone, changing the one-edge port for a port across the full feed width moves the edge-fed resonance from 5.27 to 5.41 GHz, 2.7 %. NobuRF's default port is openEMS's one-edge port, and it has the feed-wide port as an option. Any S11 comparison between two solvers has to say which port it used.

Every number here, with the scripts that produced it and the openEMS script, is in the validation report. NobuRF exports Touchstone files, so you can put its S-parameters next to an openEMS run of your own board.
